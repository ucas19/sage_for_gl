\section{gl(3|2)}
\subsection{degree of atypicality = 2}

\begin{theorem}
  Let $a,b,c,d \in \mathbb{Z}$ and $\lambda = (a,b,c|b,c)$. The projective objects $P_{\lambda}$ in $\mathfrak{B}_{a}$ have the following Verma flag formulae.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof} The Cases are as follows:\par
\begin{enumerate}
  \item $a>b>c$
    \begin{enumerate}
      \item $b>c+1 ,a>b+1$ \par
        Let $\mu:=\lambda + (0,1,1,0,0) $. 
        \begin{equation}\notag
          \begin{aligned}
            P_{\mu} = M_{a,b+1,c+1|b,c}
+M_{a,b+1,c+1|c,b}
          \end{aligned}
        \end{equation}
        \begin{equation}\notag
          \begin{aligned}
            Pr_{\lambda}(P_\mu \otimes V_6) &= 
M_{a,b,c|b,c}
+M_{a,b,c+1|b,c+1}
+M_{a,b+1,c|b+1,c}\\
                                            &
+M_{a,b+1,c+1|b+1,c+1}
+M_{a,b,c|c,b}
+M_{a,b,c+1|c+1,b}\\
                                            &
+M_{a,b+1,c|c,b+1}
+M_{a,b+1,c+1|c+1,b+1} \\
                                            &=
\sum M_{a,b,c|b,c}
+\sum M_{a,b,c+1|b,c+1}\\
                                            &
+\sum M_{a,b+1,c|b+1,c}
+\sum M_{a,b+1,c+1|b+1,c+1}
          \end{aligned}
        \end{equation} 

In all cases considered in this paper, unless stated otherwise, the weight $\lambda$ is a minimal weight in the standard filtration of the projection. Under these premises, \cref{lemma:2} guarantees that the module $P_\lambda$ must appear in the projection, constituting a direct summand. Therefore, this point will not be reiterated in the subsequent discussion. 

Consider a Verma module $M_\beta$ that appears in the projection $\prl(P_\mu \otimes V)$ but cannot be definitively established as a composition factor of $P_\lambda$ through direct application of \cref{prop:1}. Such modules require individual analysis. Furthermore, if \cref{prop:1} establishes that $(P_\lambda:M_\beta)>0$, but $M_\beta$ occurs with multiplicity $n>1$ in $\prl(P_\mu \otimes V)$, then precisely $n-1$ copies of $M_\beta$ require separate determination beyond what \cref{prop:1} provides.

We collect all such indeterminate composition factors into a set denoted by $U$. Since $P_\lambda$ is guaranteed to be a direct summand of $\prl(P_\mu \otimes V)$, our objective is to verify that all elements of $U$ belong to $P_\lambda$. To accomplish this, we employ the following methodology:

Suppose $\prl(P_\mu \otimes V_6)$ contains, besides $P_\lambda$, another indecomposable projective module $P_\beta$ as a direct summand. Then the following conditions must hold:

    (1) $M_\beta$ must appear in set $U$; \par
    (2) All composition factors of $P_\beta$ must be contained in $U$.

Using \cref{prop:1}, we compute the necessary composition factors of $P_\beta$ (though this may not yield the complete structure of $P_\beta$). If any composition factor determined by \cref{prop:1} to be in $P_\beta$ is absent from set $U$, then $P_\beta$ cannot be a direct summand of $\prl(P_\mu \otimes V_6)$.

By systematically applying this criterion to all potential projective modules $P_\beta$ corresponding to weights $\beta$ in set $U$, we can establish whether any $P_\beta$ could be a direct summand. If we can definitively exclude all possible $P_\beta$ as direct summands of $\prl(P_\mu \otimes V_6)$, then it follows that all elements of $U$ must belong to $P_\lambda$, and consequently:
\begin{equation}\notag
  \begin{aligned}
    \prl(P_\mu \otimes V_6) = P_\lambda
  \end{aligned}
\end{equation}

This approach provides a rigorous framework for determining the complete structure of $P_\lambda$ through elimination of alternative direct summands.
%Crucially, for each undetermined Verma module $M_*$, the calculation forces the existence of specific Verma modules in the standard filtration of its projective cover $P_*$ that are \textbf{not} contained in $\operatorname{Filt}(\prl(P_\lambda \otimes V_6))$ (see the third column of \cref{tab:verma-analysis_1}). This contradiction eliminates all potential additional direct summands, forcing the conclusion that $P_\lambda  =\prl(P_\mu \otimes V_6)$.

In the present case, we have $U = \{M_{a,b+1,c+1|b+1,c+1},M_{a,b+1,c+1|c+1,b+1}\}$ by \cref{prop:1}.
Following this methodology, our computational results are summarized in \cref{tab:verma-analysis_1}. As evidenced in the table, for each candidate projective module $P_\beta$ with $M_\beta \in U$, the set $\operatorname{Filt}(P_\beta) \setminus \operatorname{Filt}(\prl(P_\mu \otimes V_6))$ (determined via \cref{prop:1}) is non-empty. This non-emptiness demonstrates that none of the potential $P_\beta$ can be direct summands of $\prl(P_\mu \otimes V_6)$.

Consequently, since all alternative projective summands have been systematically excluded, we conclude definitively that: $\prl(P_\mu \otimes V_6) = P_\lambda$.
